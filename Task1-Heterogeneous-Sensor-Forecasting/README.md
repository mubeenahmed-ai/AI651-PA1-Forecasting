## What was implemented

All three are in the cells the handout designates. Nothing else in the notebook was changed, apart from the handout filename in the first cell and the two deployment choices.

1. **`RawAttentionForecaster.forward`** (Part 1): circular-conv embedding `[B,1,L] → [B,d,L]`, transpose, add the learned position table, two encoder blocks, `LayerNorm`, flatten `[B, L·d]`, linear head to `[B, H]`.
2. **`SeriesDecomposition.forward`** (Part 2, Eq. 3): pads each end by replicating the first/last sample `q = (m−1)/2` times, then applies a width-`m` average pool along time. Returns `(x − trend, trend)`, so the reconstruction is exact by construction.
3. **`aggregate_delays`** (Part 3, Eq. 6): for each of the K selected delays, builds the source index `(t − τ_j) mod L` per example and gathers along time. The result is weighted by `α_j` and summed. Delays and weights are per example and shared across heads and features. The operation stays differentiable in both values and weights. The worked example gives `z₀ = 35`, confirming the shift direction.

**Deployment choices** (Response 4c) were written from the validation-only summary *before* Output 4.3 was ever executed: **Period-routed ridge** for both populations. `run_notebook.py --until-deployment` produced that summary; the choices were then committed to the notebook and the full notebook re-run.

## Robustness and theory checks

**Seed robustness** (`audit/seed_check.py`, model seeds 0–2, same data; validation MSE, mean ± std):

| Model | Established | Held-out |
|---|---:|---:|
| Raw Attention | 0.192 ± 0.012 | 0.415 ± 0.046 |
| Attention + decomposition | 0.071 ± 0.017 | 0.162 ± 0.030 |
| Raw delay mixer | 0.042 ± 0.006 | 0.098 ± 0.018 |
| Autoformer-inspired | 0.036 ± 0.004 | 0.057 ± 0.010 |
| Period-routed ridge | 0.028 (deterministic) | 0.030 |

The ordering is identical on every seed. The 2×2 stays sub-additive: mean gains −0.121 (decomposition) and −0.150 (delay), against −0.156 combined. The submitted notebook uses model seed 0, the harness default.

**Checks against the generative model:**
- **Filter response.** A width-m moving average passes a fraction |sin(πm/P) / (m·sin(π/P))| of a period-P cycle into the trend. Over P = 14–36, widths 17/25 absorb 37% / 18% of the vibration on average (67% at P = 36); widths 33/41 absorb 12–14%. All widths capture 95–99% of the 240-sample drift. This matches Output 2.1 (residual std rises 0.58 → 1.57 with width; the width-17 "trend" visibly carries the Product C cycle) and the recovery ordering in Output 2.2.
- **Drift dominance.** Over one 96-sample context the drift spans up to 9.1 m/s² peak-to-peak, against 3.5 for the vibration. That explains why raw period recovery is only 0.69 and why the raw curve in Output 3.2 has no peak at P.
- **Recurrence.** A sinusoid's circular autocorrelation is cos(2πτ/P). Output 3.2 shows peaks at 16 and 32 (P ≈ 16.1) and troughs near P/2, as predicted.
- **Linear equivariance.** Anchored ridge maps are linear, so their error scales with vibration amplitude. Shared ridge's per-station RMSE follows this: 0.731 at amplitude 1.75 predicts 0.856 at 2.05 (observed 0.833) and 0.898 at 2.15 (Station 4, observed 0.876). Period-routed ridge is flat across stations (0.159–0.173) because it conditions on pace, not identity.
- **Held-out penalty.** The neural models degrade on Station 4 by more than amplitude scaling predicts (Raw Attention: 0.45 expected, 0.62 observed). They are not scale-equivariant and were fitted to only three stations. Autoformer-inspired degrades least (0.20 → 0.24).
- **Shared-ridge compromise (Output 1.2).** Shared and sensor-specific ridge forecasts are damped in amplitude and averaged in phase. That is the MSE-optimal average over incompatible phase advances.
- **Horizon (Output 4.2).** Errors grow with the horizon as phase errors accumulate. The noise floor is σ = 0.09, and period-routed ridge's step-1 RMSE is 0.11.

## Audit summary

The harness is leakage-safe, and the 2×2 comparison changes only the two switches: the Part 1/2 classes are bit-identical to the unified network. The main caveats for interpreting results:
- Neural checkpoints are selected on the same validation split used for model comparison (A1).
- The decomposition width is chosen by an oracle criterion at the edge of its grid (A2).
- Output 2.1 plots the worst-case window, not a typical one (A3).
